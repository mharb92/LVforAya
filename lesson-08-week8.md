## Lesson 1: Getting Around (pt 2)

### Grammar — Contraction of "3ala"
"3ala" (على, "on"/"to") contracts with a following possessive-suffixed or definite noun into "3a-"/"3al-"/"3as-" etc.:

| Contracted (Arabic) | Contracted (Rom.) | Uncontracted (Arabic) | Uncontracted (Rom.) | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| عَبيتي | 3a-beeti | على بيتي | 3ala beeti | on/to my house |
| عالُغرفة | 3al-ghurfe | على الُغرفة | 3ala íl-ghurfe | on/to the room |
| عالسَّيّارَة | 3as-sayyaara | على السَّيّارَة | 3ala ís-sayyaara | on/to the car |

## Conversation Group — New Greetings & Goodbyes

### Greeting Response
Introduction (verbatim from source): "In the last few weeks, you've learned several greetings and goodbyes. Today, we'll add a couple more to the list."

This is a new response for كيفَك (kiifak) or شو أخبارَك (shu akhbaarak):

| Arabic | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| ماشي حالي | maashi 7aali | All is going well |
| ماشي الحال | maashi íl-7aal | All is going well |

Source note (verbatim, condensed): The word ماشي (maashi) is a participle that literally means "is walking," or more figuratively, "is going along." الحال (íl-7aal) is "the state/condition," while حالي (7aali) is "my state/condition." Taken together, this phrase means "the condition is going along" or "my condition is going along." In other words, "I'm doing well."

*(Both forms — maashi 7aali and maashi íl-7aal — are given side-by-side in the source without an N/S tag; recorded as equally valid variants rather than a Northern/Southern pair.)*

### New Goodbye
| Arabic | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| مِنحكي! | mní7ki | Talk Soon! |

Source note (verbatim, condensed): This literally translates to "we talk" but is colloquially used to mean "we'll talk soon" when saying goodbye to someone. It's often combined with يَلّا (yalla) — as in: يَلّا، مِنحكي (yalla, mní7ki).

- Example Sentence Cheatsheet — Lesson 1, "Coming & Going" item 1 has a stray trailing English fragment ("It's a living room.") that does not belong to this sentence — recorded as a likely copy/paste or OCR artifact from a different lesson's material; only the correct gloss ("The girl is going to school.") was used in the table.
