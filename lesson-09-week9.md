## Lesson 1: Plurals

### Grammar Explanation — Plural Pronouns

This lesson introduces the three plural personal pronouns, extending the singular pronoun set taught in Lesson 5 (ana, ínta/ínti, huwwe/hiyye):
- **"they"** — humme (S) / hínne (N). Southern-primary rule: *humme* is canonical; *hínne* (N) is an accepted alternate.
- **"you (plural)"** — íntu. One form covers both genders; no N/S split given in source.
- **"we"** — ní7na (N) / í7na (S). Southern-primary rule: *í7na* is canonical; *ní7na* (N) is an accepted alternate.

### Grammar Explanation — Participle Agreement (Masculine / Feminine / Plural)

This is the core new grammar point of Lesson 9: participles (the verb-like descriptive words used for states/actions such as "wearing," "seeing," "going," "coming") now show **three-way agreement** — masculine singular, feminine singular, and plural (one plural form covers both genders) — rather than just the two-way masculine/feminine agreement taught previously. The general pattern:
- Masculine singular: base form (consonant ending), e.g., *laabes* (wearing).
- Feminine singular: base + **-e**, e.g., *laabse*.
- Plural (m & f): base + **-iin**, e.g., *laabsiin*.

The source gives full conjugation tables for four participles illustrating this pattern:

## Lesson 2: Where Do You Live?

### Grammar Explanation — Location & Living Sentences, and the fi-/bi- Southern Preference

Lesson 2 teaches how to describe where someone lives and where they're from, using a subject + participle (*3aayesh*/*saaken*, agreeing m/f/pl per the pattern above) + preposition + place-name structure, optionally extended with *mín* ("from," for origin) and *ma3* ("with," for accompaniment).

**Explicit Southern-dialect rule (canonical for this lesson, called out directly in the Conversation Group section of the source):** In Southern Levantine, use **فِ (fi-)** instead of **بِ (bi-)** for "in/at." This is the same recurring bi-/fi- Southern-variant pattern already noted for "in the house" (bíl-beet vs. fíl-beet) back in [[lesson-06-week6|Lesson 6]] — Lesson 9 generalizes it to all locative "in ___" constructions (fi-amriika, fíl-urdun, fi-wilaayet..., fi-madiinet...). Per the project's Southern-primary rule, **fi- forms are canonical/primary** for judging; bi- forms are accepted Northern alternates.

Location vocabulary is layered: *balad* (country) → *wilaaye* (state) → *madiine* (city), with *muqaaTa3a* (province) as an additional term given later in the Conversation Group. The instructor's introduction explicitly encourages naming all three levels (country, state, city) to build sentence complexity, and extending to the origin of one's parents/spouse/partner using *ahil* + possessive (idafa).

**Idafa/possessive note:** *ahil* ("parents") combines with a possessive suffix or a following noun to form idafa chains: *ahli* ("my parents"), *ahíl marti* ("my wife's parents"), *ahíl Saa7bi* ("my boyfriend's parents"). This is the same noun-noun/possessive-suffix idafa pattern taught with objects/animals in [[lesson-06-week6|Lesson 6]] and rooms/furniture in Lesson 7, now applied to family relations.

## Extraction notes / decisions

- **humme (S) / hínne (N)** for "they," and **í7na (S) / ní7na (N)** for "we" — explicitly tagged in the source vocab box. Southern forms (humme, í7na) are canonical per the project's Southern-primary rule; hínne/ní7na are accepted Northern alternates. íntu ("you, plural") has no N/S split in source.
- **fi- vs. bi-** for "in/at" is explicitly called out as a Southern-dialect preference in the Conversation Group box (verbatim: "In Southern Levantine, use فِ (fi-) instead of بِ (bi-)"), matching the build prompt's specific flag for this lesson. Per Southern-primary rule, fi- is canonical/primary for judging; bi- is the accepted Northern alternate. This generalizes the same bi-/fi- pattern already seen with bíl-beet/fíl-beet in [[lesson-06-week6|Lesson 6]].
- The participle agreement table (masc/fem/plural, e.g. laabes/laabse/laabsiin) is the lesson's central new grammar point and is fully captured with all four conjugated example-verbs from the source (wearing, seeing, going, coming).
