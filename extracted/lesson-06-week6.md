## Lesson 1: Possessives (pt 2)

### Grammar — Possessive Endings

With Masculine Noun (íl-beet — "the house"):
| Arabic | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| بيتي | beeti | my house |
| بيتَك | beetak | your (m) house |
| بيتِك | beetek | your (f) house |
| بيتُه | beeto | his house |
| بيتا (N) / بيتها (S) | beeta (N) / beetha (S) | her house |

With Feminine Noun (ís-sayyaara — "the car"):
| Arabic | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| سَيّارتي | sayyaarti | my car |
| سَيّارِتَك | sayyaartak | your (m) car |
| سَيّارِتِك | sayyaartek | your (f) car |
| سَيّارتُه | sayyaarto | his car |
| سَيّارتا (N) / سَيّارتها (S) | sayyaarta (N) / sayyaartha (S) | her car |

*Southern-primary rule: beetha, sayyaartha (S) canonical for "her ___"; beeta, sayyaarta (N) accepted alternates.*

### Example Sentence Cheatsheet — Lesson 1
1. ínti 3índek kalb? aah, 3índi kalb, u-3índi bísse. (S) — "Do you (f) have a dog? Yes, I have a dog, and I have a cat."
2. 3índa sayyaara 7amra. sayyaarta 7ílwe. (N) — "She has a red car. Her car is nice."
3. ír-ríjjaal za3laan. ma 3índo beet. (N) — "The man is sad. He does not have a house."
4. huwwe Tawiil, u-kalbo kbiir. — "He is tall, and his dog is big."
5. hiyye mabsuuTa. 3índha bísse hílwe. — "She is happy. She has a nice cat." *(note: source spells "hílwe" here vs "7ílwe" elsewhere — same word, likely a source typo dropping the "7"; treat as 7ílwe.)*
6. 3índak beet? La2, 3índi sha22a. (m) — "Do you (m) have a house? NO, I have an apartment."

*(Note: cheatsheet item 2/3 use N-tagged (N) forms 3índa/sayyaarta and ír-ríjjaal, consistent with Week 4's N/S tagging of ríjjaal as Northern for "man" — zalame remains Southern-canonical per Lesson 4 notes.)*

### Example Sentence Cheatsheet — Lesson 2

**Group 1:**
1. hiyye 3índa kalb. kalba bínni, u-aswad, u-abyaD. — "She has a dog. Her dog is brown, and black, and white."
2. ísh-shaab laabís kanze beeDa. sayyaarto rmaadiyye. (N) — "The guy is wearing a white T-shirt. His car is grey."
3. fii bísse beeDa u-bísse sooda. — "There is a white cat, and a black cat."
4. ínte shaayíf íl-beet? huwwe abyaD, u-rmaadi, u-bínni. — "Do you (m) see the house? It's white, and grey, and brown."

**Group 2:**
1. huwwe 3índo sayyara 2adiime, bas bíddo sayyaara jdiide. — "He has an old car, but he wants a new car."
2. íl-bint 3índha bísse beeDa Sghiire, bas bídha kalb. — "The girl has a white small cat, but she wants a dog."
3. 3índi sha22a, bas sha2ti zghiire. ana bíddi beet kbiir. — "I have an apartment, but my apartment is small. I want a big house." *(note: "zghiire" here vs "Sghiire" above — same word, capitalization/spelling variant in source, both recorded)*
4. ínti maa bíddek ísh-shanTa íl-7amra? La2, bíddi ísh-shanTa ís-sooda — "Don't you (f) want the red bag? No, I want the black bag."

### Do You Have a Pet?
| Function | Arabic | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question | عِنْدَك حَيْوان بِالبيت؟ | 3índak 7aywaan bíl-beet? | Do you have a pet? (lit. "do you have an animal in the house") |
| Response 1 | الء، ما عَندي | la2, ma 3andi | no, I don't have (one) |
| Response 2 | أي، عِندي كَلب / بِسّة | ee, 3índi kalb / bísse | yes, I have a dog / cat |

**Southern variant note:** In Southern Levantine, بِالبيت (bíl-beet) is instead فِالبيت (fíl-beet). Per project's Southern-primary rule, fíl-beet is canonical/primary for judging; bíl-beet (N) is accepted alternate. This is the same bíl-/fil- Southern-variant pattern the build prompt calls out.

## Extraction notes / decisions
- "bísse" given as the general/all-dialect term for "cat" in Lesson 1 vocab card; "2aTTa" (Syria) and "bsayne" (Lebanon) given as regional synonyms, not tagged N/S — per project's Southern-primary rule and since bísse is used throughout example phrases/cheatsheets without regional tag, treat bísse as canonical/primary, with 2aTTa and bsayne as additional accepted regional alternates (not strictly "Northern," but same function).
- "she has"/"she wants" (3índha/bídha vs 3índa/bídda) and "shoes" (shuuz vs booT) are the clearest explicit N/S tagged pairs this lesson — Southern forms (3índha, bídha, shuuz) are canonical per project rule.
- "bíl-beet" (N) vs "fíl-beet" (S) for "in the house" explicitly noted in Conversation Group — this is the same Southern-preference-for-"fi-" pattern the build prompt flags as recurring in Week 9 too. fíl-beet is canonical.
- Cheatsheet item 5 (Lesson 1) spells "hílwe" without the "7" — recorded as source typo/inconsistency, normalize to "7ílwe" (nice/pretty, f) per canonical romanization elsewhere in the same lesson.
- Cheatsheet item 3 (Lesson 2, Group 2) uses "zghiire" vs the vocab-table/example-phrase spelling "Sghiire" — same word (small, f), spelling inconsistency in source, both forms should be accepted as matching variants in fuzzy-matching logic, canonical form "Sghiire."
- Bonus Exercise paragraphs have no per-sentence English gloss or word-by-word romanization in source (full Arabic paragraphs only, contrasted N vs S) — English gloss above is constructed from this lesson's + prior lessons' vocab tables for app-build usability, flagged as not verbatim-sourced, consistent with the same flagging approach used for Lesson 4's Reading Exercise.
- Group Exercise images are visual-only prompts (no text/answer key in source) — recorded as descriptive prompt list for open-ended production drills, consistent with how Lesson 5's Group Exercise visual prompts were handled.
