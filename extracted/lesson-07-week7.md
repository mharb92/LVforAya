### Grammar — Noun-Noun "IDafa" Pairings (Masculine)
Shows the "A ___" vs. "the ___'s ___" construction for masculine nouns (definite article moves to the second/possessor noun):

| Arabic (A) | Rom. (A) | English (A) | Arabic (The) | Rom. (The) | English (The) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| قَميص وَلَد | 2amiiS walad | a boy's shirt | قَميص الوَلَد | 2amiiS íl-walad | the boy's shirt |
| بيت مَرَة | beet mara | a woman's house | بيت المَرَة | beet íl-mara | the woman's house |
| بوط شَب | booT shab | a man's shoes | بوط الَّشب | booT ísh-shab | the man's shoes |

*Note: "shab" here (guy/young man) matches "shaab" as spelled in Week 6's Lesson 2 example phrase ("ísh-shaab laabís kanze beeDa") — same word, single-vowel vs. double-vowel spelling variant; both recorded as matching.*

### Example Sentence Cheatsheet — Lesson 1
1. ana shaayfe ghírfe. hiyye ghírfet 2a3de. bíl-ghírfe fii tílfízyoon aswad, u-shíbbaak kbiir, u-Suufaaye kbiire. loon íS-Suufaaye bínni, u-aswad. fii kírsi? la2, maa fii kírsi. (N) — "I (f) see a room. It's a living room. In the living room there is a black TV, and a big window, and a big sofa. The color of the sofa is brown, and black. Is there a chair? No, there is no chair."
2. ana shaayíf ghurfet noom. hiyye ghurfet walad. loon íl-ghurfe, azra2, u-bínni. juwwa íl-ghurfe fii takhít kbiir, u-kanabaaye zghiire. maa fii maktab fíl-ghurfe. (S) — "I (m) see a bedroom. It's a boy's room. [The color of the room is blue, and brown.] Inside the room, there is a big bed, and a small sofa. There is no desk in the room."

| Arabic (Object) | Rom. (Object) | English (Object) | Arabic (IDafa) | Rom. (IDafa) | English (IDafa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| غِرفة | ghírfe | room | غِرفِة النّوم | ghírfet ín-noom | the bedroom |
| بلُوزة | bluuze | T-shirt | بلُوزة البِنت | bluuzet íl-bínt | the girl's T-shirt |
| سَيّارَة | sayyaara | car | سَيّارِة الَّزَلمة | sayyaaret íz-zalame | the man's car |

### Example Sentence Cheatsheet — Lesson 2
1. Fii ríjjaal. huwwa laabís 2amiiS azra2, u-booT aswad. 3índo beet jdiid. Beeto zghiir. loon íl-beet abyaD. (N) — "There is a man, He is wearing a blue shirt, and a black shoes. He has a new house. His house is small. The color of the house is white."
2. bi-ghírfet íl-2a3de, 3índo Suufaaye, u-tílfízyuun, u-Taawle zghiire. (N) — "In the living room, he has a sofa, a TV, and a small table."
3. fi-ghurfet ín-noom 3índo takhít, u-kursi, u-maktab. u-fii kombuter kamaan. (S) — "In the bed room he has a bed, a chair, and a desk. and there is a computer too."
4. 3índo 7ammaam zghiir, u-juwwa íl-7ammaam, fii twaaleet, u-dush. (S) — "He has a small bathroom, and inside the bathroom, there is a toilet and a shower."
5. u-3índo kamaan maTbakh. loono azra2. fíl-maTbakh, maa fii Taawle, u-maa fii karaasi. (S) — "And he has also a kitchen. It's color is blue, there is no table, and there are no chairs."

## Extraction notes / decisions
- This lesson's source gives explicit N/S dual romanization for nearly all vocab and example-phrase content (more so than Weeks 4–6). Every N/S pair recorded above treats S as canonical/primary per project rule, N as accepted alternate — this is the most heavily dual-tagged lesson so far.
- Example Phrases (Lesson 1) are presented in the source as two side-by-side dialect boxes (a Southern-tagged room-image box and a Northern-tagged room-image box) rather than a single shared list — recorded as two separate tables (S box, N box) to preserve that pairing, since the S and N boxes describe two *different* rooms/scenarios (bedroom vs. living room), not the same sentence in two dialects.
- Cheatsheet item 2 (Lesson 1) includes the line "loon íl-ghurfe, azra2, u-bínni" but the source's English translation block for that cheatsheet entry omits a corresponding gloss line (only "It's a boy's room," "Inside the room...," and "There is no desk..." are given). Recorded the Arabic/romanization verbatim and supplied the literal translation "The color of the room is blue, and brown" in the sentence-cheatsheet entry (bracketed) since it is directly derivable from established vocab (loon=color, azra2=blue, bínni=brown), flagged as not verbatim-sourced.
- "maktab" appears twice with two different meanings: "desk" (Lesson 1 vocab) and "office" (Lesson 2 vocab) — both recorded as separate vocab entries under the same Arabic/romanization, since the source itself introduces both senses without comment; treated as a real source ambiguity rather than an error.
- "bluuze" (T-shirt) is introduced only inside the Lesson 2 feminine-IDafa example table, with no standalone vocab card — included as a vocab item since it's new content, with a note on where it was found.
- IDafa masculine example "booT shab / booT ísh-shab" spells "shab" (guy) with a single vowel, while Week 6 spells the same word "shaab" (ísh-shaab) — recorded as a spelling variant of the same word, both forms noted as equivalent.
- Cheatsheet item 1 (Lesson 2)'s English gloss reads "a black shoes" (article/number mismatch) — preserved verbatim from source as a source-side English translation quirk, not an Arabic content issue.
- The Suufaaye/kanabaaye (couch) and duush/dush (shower) N/S tags are given via small colored icon markers in the source vocab boxes (green circle = N, red flag = S) rather than inline "(N)"/"(S)" text; inferred and cross-confirmed against matching tags used in the same-lesson example-phrase cheatsheets (e.g., "kanabaaye zghiire" appears in the (S)-tagged sentence, "Suufaaye kbiire" in the (N)-tagged sentence).
- Group Exercise is a single set of 20 distinct furnished-room images (not grouped into categories of 5 identical-type images like Week 6's exercise) — described individually above as visual-only prompts for open-ended in-class production, consistent with how Week 6's Group Exercise visual prompts were handled (no text/answer key exists in source).
- No "Bonus Exercise" or reading exercise is present in this file (unlike Week 4 and Week 6) — Week 7 consists of Lesson 1, Lesson 2, one Conversation Group (Review), and one Group Exercise; nothing else in the source was left unaccounted for.
